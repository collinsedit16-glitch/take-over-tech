Teens from London built an app to help the homeless

By TakeOver Staff  |  18 April 2025  |  4 min read

While most teenagers are glued to their phones playing games or scrolling social media, three Year 11 students have been busy building one. The team known as Absolute Zero took first prize at the National Youth Tech Summit yesterday, impressing judges with their climate-change app ShelterCover.

The app helps food banks distribute leftover meals to people in need, connecting surplus food directly with homeless shelters in real time. The idea came after one of the team members volunteered at a local food bank and noticed how much food was being wasted every week.

"We didn't just want to sit around. We wanted to use tech for good — to help the world be a better, safer, more positive place for everyone."

How the app works

ShelterCover uses a simple map interface to show nearby food banks and shelters. Volunteers can log surplus food and shelters can claim it in real time. The app was built using React Native and is available on both Android and iOS devices.

The team say they hope to roll the app out to 50 London food banks by September 2025. They are also planning a school talk series to encourage more young people to use technology for social good.
